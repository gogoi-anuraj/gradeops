# 5.6 Common Forces

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this section, you will be able to:
- Define normal and tension forces

---

Forces are given many names, such as push, pull, thrust, and weight. Traditionally, forces have been grouped into several categories and given names relating to their source, how they are transmitted, or their effects.

---

## A Catalog of Forces: Normal, Tension, and Other Examples

### Normal Force
The force supporting the weight of an object, or a load, that is perpendicular to the surface of contact between the load and its support is defined as a **normal force** ($\vec{\mathbf{N}}$). The word *normal* means perpendicular to a surface.

- **On a Horizontal Surface**:
  - Vector Form: $\vec{\mathbf{N}} = -m\vec{\mathbf{g}}$
  - Scalar Form: $N = mg$
- **On an Incline**:
  - The normal force is perpendicular to the inclined surface and equals the perpendicular component of weight:
    $$N = mg \cos\theta$$

### Example 5.12: Weight on an Incline

Consider a skier on a slope inclined at $25^\circ$. Her total mass including equipment is $60.0\text{ kg}$.  
(a) What is her acceleration if friction is negligible?  
(b) What is her acceleration if friction is $45.0\text{ N}$?

![Figure 5.22](figure_5_22_placeholder.png)  
*Figure 5.22 Free-body diagram for a skier on an incline.*

#### Strategy
Choose a coordinate system with the x-axis parallel to the slope (downslope positive) and the y-axis perpendicular to the slope.  
Decompose the weight into components:
- Parallel component: $w_x = w \sin 25^\circ = mg \sin 25^\circ$
- Perpendicular component: $w_y = w \cos 25^\circ = mg \cos 25^\circ$

#### Solution

**(a) Neglecting Friction ($f = 0$):**
$$a_x = \frac{F_{\text{net},x}}{m} = \frac{mg \sin 25^\circ}{m} = g \sin 25^\circ$$
$$a_x = (9.80\text{ m/s}^2)(0.4226) = 4.14\text{ m/s}^2$$

**(b) Including Friction ($f = 45.0\text{ N}$):**
$$a_x = \frac{F_{\text{net},x}}{m} = \frac{mg \sin 25^\circ - f}{m}$$
$$a_x = \frac{(60.0\text{ kg})(9.80\text{ m/s}^2)(0.4226) - 45.0\text{ N}}{60.0\text{ kg}} = 3.39\text{ m/s}^2$$

#### Significance
In the absence of friction, all objects slide down an incline of angle $\theta$ with acceleration $a = g \sin\theta$, regardless of mass.

### Weight Components on an Incline

When an object rests on an incline at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal:

$$egin{aligned}
w_x &= w \sin\theta = mg \sin\theta \quad \text{(Parallel to incline, causes downslope motion)} \
w_y &= w \cos\theta = mg \cos\theta \quad \text{(Perpendicular to incline)}
\end{aligned}$$

> **Check Your Understanding 5.8**  
> A force of $1150\text{ N}$ acts parallel to a ramp to push a $250\text{-kg}$ gun safe into a moving van ($17^\circ$ ramp).  
> - **(a)** Without friction:  
>   $$F_{\text{net}} = 1150\text{ N} - mg \sin 17^\circ = 1150 - (250)(9.80)(0.2924) = 1150 - 716.3 = 433.7\text{ N}$$  
>   $$a = \frac{433.7\text{ N}}{250\text{ kg}} = 1.73\text{ m/s}^2$$
> - **(b)** With friction ($f = 120\text{ N}$):  
>   $$F_{\text{net}} = 433.7\text{ N} - 120\text{ N} = 313.7\text{ N}$$  
>   $$a = \frac{313.7\text{ N}}{250\text{ kg}} = 1.25\text{ m/s}^2$$

### Tension
**Tension** is a pulling force that acts along the length of a stretched flexible connector (such as a rope, cable, or tendon).

- Flexible connectors can only exert a pull parallel to their length ("you can't push a rope").
- For a mass $m$ suspended at rest:  
  $$T = w = mg$$

#### Transmitting Forces Around Corners
Flexible connectors transmit force magnitude undiminished (if frictionless), changing only its direction (e.g., tendons in fingers, bicycle brake cables).

---

### Example 5.13: What Is the Tension in a Tightrope?

Calculate the tension in a wire supporting a $70.0\text{-kg}$ tightrope walker where the wire sags by $\theta = 5.0^\circ$.

![Figure 5.26](figure_5_26_placeholder.png)  
*Figure 5.26 Tightrope walker causing a wire to sag by $5.0^\circ$.*

#### Solution
For a centered load sagging at angle $\theta$:

$$F_{\text{net},y} = 2T \sin\theta - w = 0 \implies T = \frac{w}{2\sin\theta} = \frac{mg}{2\sin\theta}$$

Substituting values:

$$T = \frac{(70.0\text{ kg})(9.80\text{ m/s}^2)}{2 \sin 5.0^\circ} = \frac{686\text{ N}}{2(0.08716)} = 3935\text{ N} \approx 3930\text{ N}$$

#### Significance
Because $\theta$ is small, the tension in the wire ($3930\text{ N}$) is almost six times the walker's weight ($686\text{ N}$).

> **General Formula for Perpendicular Force on a Flexible Connector:**  
> $$T = \frac{F_\perp}{2\sin\theta}$$

> **Check Your Understanding 5.9**  
> A $3.0\text{-m}$ rope is pulled at its midpoint by $F_\perp = 200.0\text{ N}$, displacing it $0.25\text{ m}$.  
> - Half-length $L = 1.5\text{ m}$, displacement $y = 0.25\text{ m} \implies \sin\theta = \frac{0.25}{1.5} = 0.1667$.  
> - Force on car (Tension $T$):  
>   $$T = \frac{F_\perp}{2\sin\theta} = \frac{200.0\text{ N}}{2(0.1667)} = 600\text{ N}$$

### Friction & Spring Force

- **Friction**: A resistive force opposing motion or its tendency.
- **Spring Force**: A restoring force proportional to displacement from the relaxed state (Hooke's Law):
  $$\vec{\mathbf{F}} = -k\vec{\mathbf{x}} \tag{5.11}$$
  where $k$ is the spring stiffness constant and $\vec{\mathbf{x}}$ is displacement ($x = 0$ at relaxed position).

---

## Real Forces and Inertial Frames

- **Real Forces**: Forces with a physical origin (e.g., gravitational pull, electromagnetic force).
- **Fictitious Forces**: Apparent forces arising because the observer is in an accelerating or noninertial frame of reference (e.g., rotating frame).  
  *Test*: If you cannot identify an equal and opposite reaction force, the force is fictitious.
- **Inertial Frame**: A frame of reference where all forces are real and Newton's laws hold without modification. Earth is approximately an inertial frame, though large-scale phenomena (e.g., hurricanes, Coriolis effect) demonstrate noninertial characteristics.
